Apple Inc. was founded on April 1, 1976, by Steve Jobs, Steve Wozniak, and Ronald Wayne in Los Altos, California. The company's first product was the Apple I, a hand-built computer kit designed primarily by Wozniak. The company was originally named Apple Computer, Inc. and dropped "Computer" from its name in January 2007 to reflect its expansion beyond personal computers into consumer electronics. Apple is headquartered at Apple Park in Cupertino, California, a 2.8-million-square-foot campus designed by Foster + Partners that opened to employees in 2017.

Apple's early history includes the Apple II (1977), one of the first highly successful mass-produced microcomputers, and the Macintosh (1984), which popularized the graphical user interface for a mass consumer audience. Steve Jobs left Apple in 1985 following an internal power struggle and returned in 1997 after Apple acquired his company NeXT. Jobs served as interim and then permanent CEO from 1997 until 2011.

The Apple Newton, an early handheld personal digital assistant, was discontinued in 1998 as part of Jobs's post-return product line simplification. The iMac (1998), iBook (1999), iPod (2001), iTunes Store (2003), iPhone (2007), App Store (2008), iPad (2010), Apple Watch (2015), AirPods (2016), and Apple Vision Pro (2024) each opened new product categories for the company over the following decades. Apple began its transition from Intel processors to its own Apple Silicon chips in Mac computers in November 2020, completing the transition across its Mac lineup by 2023.

Tim Cook has served as Chief Executive Officer of Apple since August 24, 2011, succeeding co-founder Steve Jobs, who passed away in October 2011. Cook previously served as Apple's Chief Operating Officer, overseeing worldwide sales and operations. Kevan Parekh was named Apple's Chief Financial Officer in 2024, succeeding Luca Maestri, who had held the role since 2014 and transitioned to an advisory position before departing the company. Other members of Apple's executive team include Katherine Adams, General Counsel and Senior Vice President of Legal and Global Security; Deirdre O'Brien, Senior Vice President of Retail and People; Craig Federighi, Senior Vice President of Software Engineering; Johny Srouji, Senior Vice President of Hardware Technologies; John Ternus, Senior Vice President of Hardware Engineering; and Greg Joswiak, Senior Vice President of Worldwide Marketing.

For its fiscal year 2024, which ended September 28, 2024, Apple reported total net sales of $391.035 billion, an increase of approximately 2 percent compared to fiscal year 2023. Net income for fiscal year 2024 was $93.736 billion, and diluted earnings per share were $6.11. For comparison, fiscal year 2023, which ended September 30, 2023, had total net sales of approximately $383.285 billion and net income of approximately $96.995 billion; fiscal 2023 net income included the effect of one-time items that made year-over-year net income comparisons less straightforward than the revenue comparison.

The main contributors to fiscal 2024 revenue by product and service category were iPhone, which generated $201.183 billion; Services, which generated $96.169 billion; Wearables, Home and Accessories, which generated $37.005 billion; Mac, which generated $29.984 billion; and iPad, which generated $26.694 billion. Services revenue grew approximately 13 percent year over year and was the fastest-growing of Apple's reportable segments in fiscal 2024, while iPad revenue declined slightly compared to the prior year.

By reportable geographic segment, the Americas remained Apple's largest region by revenue in fiscal 2024, followed by Europe, Greater China, Japan, and Rest of Asia Pacific. Greater China revenue declined slightly in fiscal 2024 amid continued smartphone competition from domestic manufacturers such as Huawei and Xiaomi, along with broader macroeconomic pressure on consumer spending in the region. Japan and Rest of Asia Pacific both posted modest year-over-year growth in fiscal 2024.

Apple's gross margin for fiscal 2024 was approximately 46.2 percent, up from about 44.1 percent in fiscal 2023, driven in part by the continued growth of higher-margin Services revenue relative to hardware revenue. Products gross margin and Services gross margin are reported separately in Apple's financial disclosures, with Services carrying a substantially higher margin than the Products segment as a whole.

Research and development expense for fiscal 2024 was $31.37 billion, reflecting continued investment in custom silicon, software platforms, artificial intelligence, and new product categories such as spatial computing. Apple's R&D spending has grown considerably over the past decade as the company has expanded from primarily consumer devices into chip design, health sensors, augmented and mixed reality, and generative AI features branded as Apple Intelligence.

Apple's iPhone lineup during fiscal 2024 centered on the iPhone 15 family, released in September 2023, with the iPhone 16 family launching in September 2024, just before the fiscal year closed. The iPhone 16 introduced the Camera Control button and expanded on-device Apple Intelligence features. Apple's Mac lineup includes the MacBook Air, MacBook Pro, iMac, Mac mini, Mac Studio, and Mac Pro, all of which have transitioned to Apple Silicon chips including the M-series processors. The iPad lineup includes the base iPad, iPad Air, iPad mini, and iPad Pro, the last of which adopted OLED display technology and the M4 chip in 2024.

Apple Watch, first released in 2015, is offered in Series, SE, and Ultra variants, with health and fitness tracking as a central feature, including ECG, blood oxygen monitoring, and fall detection in various markets subject to regulatory clearance. AirPods, first released in 2016, include the base AirPods, AirPods Pro with active noise cancellation, and AirPods Max over-ear headphones. HomePod and HomePod mini serve as Apple's smart speaker products, integrating with the Siri voice assistant and HomeKit smart home ecosystem.

Apple Vision Pro, a mixed-reality headset combining passthrough video with digital overlays, launched in the United States in February 2024 at a starting price of $3,499 and expanded to additional countries including the United Kingdom, Canada, Australia, France, Germany, and Japan later in 2024. Vision Pro runs visionOS and remains a small contributor to overall company revenue relative to iPhone, Mac, and iPad.

Apple Intelligence, a suite of generative AI features integrated across iOS, iPadOS, and macOS, was announced at Apple's Worldwide Developers Conference in June 2024 and began rolling out in stages later that year, including writing tools, notification summaries, an overhauled Siri experience, and integration with third-party large language models for certain requests.

Apple's Services segment includes the App Store, Apple Music, Apple TV+, iCloud, AppleCare, Apple Pay, Apple Card, Apple Arcade, Apple News+, and Apple Fitness+. The App Store hosts millions of third-party applications and generates revenue primarily through commissions on paid app downloads and in-app purchase transactions, typically 30 percent for most transactions and 15 percent for smaller developers and subscription renewals under Apple's Small Business Program and subscription-year-two pricing. AppleCare offers extended warranty and support plans across Apple's hardware lineup.

Apple repurchased approximately $95 billion of its own common stock during fiscal 2024 as part of its ongoing capital return program, which began in 2012. The company also paid dividends of roughly $15.2 billion to shareholders in fiscal 2024. Apple's board of directors authorized an additional $110 billion share repurchase program in May 2024, one of the largest single buyback authorizations by any public company. Since the capital return program began, Apple has returned several hundred billion dollars to shareholders through a combination of dividends and buybacks, and the company has also executed multiple stock splits historically, including a four-for-one split in 2020.

Apple's manufacturing relies heavily on a network of contract manufacturers, most prominently Foxconn (Hon Hai Precision Industry), for final assembly of iPhone and other products, and on chip foundry partners, most prominently Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company (TSMC), for fabrication of Apple's custom silicon designs. Apple has stated an intent to diversify final assembly capacity beyond China, including expanded iPhone assembly in India and continued investment in Vietnam for other product lines, though China remains central to Apple's supply chain.

Apple continues to face antitrust scrutiny in multiple jurisdictions. In March 2024, the United States Department of Justice, along with a group of state attorneys general, filed a lawsuit alleging that Apple illegally maintains a monopoly over the smartphone market through practices affecting app distribution, messaging interoperability, digital wallets, and other areas. The European Union's Digital Markets Act required Apple to make several changes to the App Store and iOS within the EU during 2024, including allowing alternative app marketplaces and alternative payment processing for iOS apps. Apple was also a defendant in the long-running Epic Games v. Apple litigation concerning App Store payment policies, which produced rulings affecting how Apple can restrict developers from directing users to outside payment options.

Apple's main competitors vary by product category. In smartphones, Apple competes primarily with Samsung and other Android device manufacturers. In personal computing, Apple competes with Windows PC makers such as Dell, HP, and Lenovo. In digital services and streaming, Apple competes with companies including Google, Amazon, Spotify, and Netflix. In wearables, competitors include Samsung, Garmin, and Fitbit (owned by Google).

Apple has committed to making its entire supply chain and product life cycle carbon neutral by 2030 under its "Apple 2030" initiative, and it reported that its corporate operations, including facilities and business travel, achieved carbon neutrality in 2020. Apple has increasingly used recycled materials in its products, including recycled aluminum in MacBook and iPad enclosures and recycled rare earth elements in certain components, and it publishes an annual Environmental Progress Report detailing these efforts.

The company employed approximately 164,000 full-time equivalent employees as of September 2024. Apple operates several hundred retail stores worldwide under the Apple Store brand, alongside its online store and a network of authorized resellers and carrier partnerships. Apple's retail strategy emphasizes in-store technical support through the Genius Bar and hands-on product experience.

Apple's effective tax rate for fiscal 2024 was affected by a one-time charge related to a 2024 European Commission state aid decision concerning Apple's historical tax arrangements in Ireland, which required Apple to make a substantial one-time payment. Foreign currency fluctuations had a modest unfavorable impact on Apple's reported revenue growth in fiscal 2024, as they have in several prior fiscal years given the large share of Apple's revenue generated outside the United States.

Warranty costs and litigation reserves are recorded as part of Apple's operating expenses and can vary from quarter to quarter depending on product mix, claims experience, and the status of ongoing legal proceedings. Apple discloses these and other contingencies in the notes to its consolidated financial statements included in its annual report on Form 10-K.

Apple typically releases its fiscal fourth-quarter and full-year results in late October or early November, with the fiscal 2024 full-year results announced on October 31, 2024. Apple's fiscal year runs from late September or early October of one calendar year to the corresponding date in the following calendar year, ending on the last Saturday of September in most years.

Apple maintains investment-grade credit ratings from major rating agencies, reflecting its substantial cash and marketable securities position alongside its debt issuance program, which the company has used opportunistically to fund shareholder returns while preserving liquidity. Apple's balance sheet has historically held tens of billions of dollars in cash, cash equivalents, and marketable securities.

Apple Park, the company's primary corporate campus, includes a large circular main building sometimes referred to as "the ring," along with a visitor center, a fitness and wellness center, and thousands of trees selected to be drought-resistant given California's climate. The campus also houses the Steve Jobs Theater, used for major product announcement events.
